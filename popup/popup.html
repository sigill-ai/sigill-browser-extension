Sigill Digital Evidence
Stamping — your content stays local

For each conversation turn, the extension works entirely in your browser:

  1. The prompt and response are combined into an evidence envelope.
  2. The envelope is canonicalised using JCS (RFC 8785) — byte-for-byte identical on any device.
  3. A SHA-256 hash is computed — a fingerprint that identifies the content without revealing it.
  4. Only the hash is sent to a RFC 3161 Timestamp Authority, which returns a signed timestamp token.
  5. The token and the full envelope are stored locally. The timestamp proves the conversation existed at that moment.

The TSA only ever sees a hash — your conversation content never leaves the browser during stamping.

Sealing — explicit upload to Sigill

When you click Seal log, the full JSONL log is uploaded to Sigill and signed with a CAdES detached signature. You receive the original log and a separate .p7s signature file. Sealing is always a deliberate action — nothing is uploaded automatically.
